Anno 1665: Printing Services of Johann Jakob Decker (I)

Printer to the University of Basel

A notice to all scholars and esteemed persons of Basel!

List of the settlement concluded between the Reverend Regent of the University of Basel and Johann Jakob Deckern (I), a printer appointed by the aforementioned university: Namely, what and how much the printer should demand and take from each sheet of Theses, proposed for public debate, or other materials and things, for printing as his remuneration, both from and foreign the Confederate states.

Anno 1665

Terms of Service

  1. That the theses of candidates or others who wish to hold a disputation or otherwise have something printed shall be delivered to him neatly, legibly, and clearly. If this is not the case, or if something new is altered in the copy, he shall be entitled, in fairness, to additional payment.
  2. That for tips (Trankgeld), as a debt, he shall not be entitled to demand anything, but it shall be left to the goodwill and discretion of those having something printed.
  3. That he shall be entitled to charge 3 schillings per sheet for printing paper, and 5 schillings per sheet for writing paper.
  4. That he shall take care especially not to delay the candidates with their work; likewise, the gentlemen deans of each faculty shall so arrange matters that he is not overwhelmed or overburdened at any one time.
  5. That he shall not be obliged to release the printed copies (whatever they may be) until he has been duly satisfied and paid for them.
  6. In the event that anyone whatsoever should give his theses, disputation-papers, programs, or similar items to be printed by another printer than the aforementioned Johann Jakob Decker (I), that person shall, according to the judgment of the Honorable Regent, have to expect the appropriate penalty.
  7. If any dispute or contention should arise between those who wish to have something printed and the said printer, the matter shall always be decided by the Magnificent Rector of the University and the dean of the respective faculty.

Basel, 1665
